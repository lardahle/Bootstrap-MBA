Like a convertible note but **not debt**: there's **no interest and no maturity date**.

- **Key Points**:
  - The terms are a valuation cap and/or a discount rate, sometimes with an MFN clause.
  - The company never has to repay it. It simply converts at the next priced round.
  - **It only benefits you if you grow the company past the valuation cap.** Below the cap, the discount still gives early investors more shares.
- **Example from lecture** (simplified, measured against the round's valuation): $1M invested with a $4M cap and a 20% discount (investors pay 80% of the round price).
  - Next round at **$10M**: converts at the $4M cap, worth about **25%**.
  - Next round still at **$4M**: the discount applies, so $1M ÷ ($4M × 0.8) ≈ **31.25%**.
- Y Combinator created the standard SAFE in 2013. Today's "post-money SAFE" calculates ownership slightly differently, so model it before you sign.
